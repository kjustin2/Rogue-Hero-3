# Visual Reference Board — Gold Slice

This board records transferable composition principles only. Rogue Hero 3 keeps
its original procedural geometry, dark-fantasy identity, palette, characters,
and effects; no third-party or generated art is copied into the game.

## Hades II — hierarchy and material separation

Official reference: https://www.supergiantgames.com/games/hades-ii/

- Preserve a bright, unmistakable player read against darker arena values.
- Use large shape hierarchy: one dominant actor mass, one weapon/action line,
  then restrained costume accents.
- Keep combat effects directional and local so the arena remains legible beneath
  the impact moment.

## Curse of the Dead Gods — darkness with readable combat space

Official reference: https://www.focus-entmt.com/en/games/curse-of-the-dead-gods

- Separate walkable floor, blocking silhouettes, and distant void into three
  value bands instead of allowing every surface to crush to black.
- Let warm practical light describe safe movement space while hostile coral
  telegraphs remain the loudest danger signal.
- Frame rooms with foreground architecture without placing decoration over the
  central combat field.

## Ravenswatch — compact co-op-scale readability

Official reference: https://ravenswatch.com/en/

- Boss scale comes from body mass, landmark framing, and camera distance—not
  from clipping the subject against screen edges.
- Read attacks first through body anticipation, then ground warning, then the
  damaging effect; all three must agree spatially.
- Keep compact HUD clusters near edges and reserve the center for actors,
  targets, routes, and threats.

## Rogue Hero 3 transfer rules

- Palette stays 70% charcoal/ink, 20% warm stone/ember, 10% emissive magic.
- Cyan is player language; coral is hostile danger; gold is critical/parry;
  green is healing; violet is Rift progression.
- Each arena plate needs foreground framing, a clean central combat field,
  midground landmarks, and distant silhouette depth.
- Each actor needs a readable front/back cue, joint hierarchy, weapon/action
  silhouette, contact grounding, and a light-catching accent.
- Cinematics stage establishing view, hero reaction, boss performance, low
  reveal, identity title, musical hit, and one deterministic handoff.
- Terminal screens show outcome, actor, rewards, and next action only; combat
  instrumentation is suppressed.
